# DrewCraft Cult Lore Bible

## Canon identity

**Official name:** The Covenant of the Closed Sky  
**Common name:** the Covenant  
**What they call DrewCraft aviators:** the Unmoored  
**Capital:** Vespera, the Rooted Crown  
**Founding saint:** Saint Orun  
**Founding catastrophe:** the Falling Fire  
**Symbol:** a downward-broken wing  
**Visual language:** blackened charcoal, slate, warm stone, aged ivory, oxblood, tarnished gold

## Core doctrine

> **Height is not sin. Severance is. A mountain may rise because it remains rooted; a person who flies abandons the earth that bears them.**

The Covenant does not believe that being physically high is itself sinful. Mountains, towers, walls, and stairs are acceptable because they remain joined to the earth. Their entire civilization is built around the distinction between **ascent** and **severance**.

This is why their hidden capital can be monumental, elevated, and almost absurdly high without contradicting their anti-aviation faith. A Covenant theologian would say that a mountain peak is still earth, while a person suspended by wings or engines has made their body dependent on empty air.

Their favorite theological phrase is:

> **The earth bears. The sky merely permits.**

## The Falling Fire

The Covenant began after a real historical aviation disaster.

The ancestors of the Covenant belonged to a larger road-building civilization that had begun experimenting with powered human flight. The first major public ascent launched multiple machines from tall towers despite rapidly worsening weather.

A violent storm broke the formation apart. Burning aircraft fell into the city below. Towers and homes caught fire. Some crews vanished into cloud and were never recovered.

The event became known as **the Falling Fire**.

The oldest surviving writings are more nuanced than later propaganda. They describe pressure changes, shifting winds, and ignored warnings rather than an obvious supernatural intervention. Saint Orun himself admits that he felt pride when he saw the first machines rise.

His conclusion was not that height is evil. It was that civilization had mistaken technological capability for independence from the conditions that sustain life.

The Covenant hardened that lesson into religion.

## Saint Orun

Orun was a surveyor and road engineer who survived the Falling Fire. His central idea was that civilization should remain **borne**—physically, socially, and morally supported by things with weight and continuity.

He wrote that roads, foundations, graves, walls, and mountains all teach the same lesson: freedom is not the absence of dependence; it is the ability to know what bears you.

Later generations canonized him as Saint Orun.

The most important line attributed to him is:

> **Climb by stair, ridge, tower, and mountain. Rise as high as the earth can carry you. Do not sever the bond.**

## The Eight Chains

After the disaster and the wars that followed, the Covenant organized its territory around eight major religious-strategic houses called **the Eight Chains**.

They are:

1. **Ashen Gate** — First Chain — Vigilance. Frontier gate-fort and patrol source. Its doctrine emphasizes vigilance, borders, the first organized defense after the Falling Fire.
2. **First Stone Abbey** — Second Chain — Memory. Abbey and pilgrimage house. Its doctrine emphasizes creation doctrine, memory, the sacredness of weight and foundations.
3. **Red Reliquary** — Third Chain — Mortality. Reliquary, crypt, and burial temple. Its doctrine emphasizes death, burial, returning the body to earth, names of the dead.
4. **Low Bell Monastery** — Fourth Chain — Obedience. Monastery built around a deep bell tower. Its doctrine emphasizes silence, discipline, pilgrimage on foot, obedience to the weight of the world.
5. **Closed Wing Bastion** — Fifth Chain — Defense. Fortress and anti-air battery. Its doctrine emphasizes military anti-aviation doctrine and defense against the Unmoored.
6. **Saint Orun Scriptorium** — Sixth Chain — Doctrine. Scriptorium, archive tower, and doctrinal school. Its doctrine emphasizes the founder's writings, censorship, the history of the Falling Fire, forbidden engines.
7. **Cairnwatch** — Seventh Chain — Measure. Survey fortress and road command. Its doctrine emphasizes maps, roads, boundaries, measured travel, the divided-waymark system.
8. **Eighth Chain Citadel** — Eighth Chain — Unity. Regional citadel and command seat. Its doctrine emphasizes unity of the eight chains, hierarchy, war orders, loyalty to Vespera.


The Chains are not literal chains. They are a political-religious promise that no region stands alone.

At the center is **Vespera, the Rooted Crown**, which is described as the place where the Eight Chains are fastened together.

## Vespera, the Rooted Crown

Vespera was built beyond the old frontier after earlier wars convinced the Covenant that its capital should not appear on ordinary maps.

The city occupies a mountain or high plateau. Its intended visual sequence is:

**outer approach -> lower walls -> steep processional roads and stairs -> eight ceremonial courts -> High Basilica -> Palace of Weight**

The city is deliberately walkable from its lowest road to its highest door.

Its monumental elevation is part of the theology: the Covenant wants to prove that humans may reach extraordinary heights without leaving the earth.

The final approach includes the **Grand Stair**, which no orthodox pilgrim is permitted to bypass using lifts or flight.

The capital's air defenses are therefore not merely military. They defend an architectural argument.

## The Covenant and aviation

The Covenant destroys or confiscates:

- fixed-wing aircraft;
- rotary-wing aircraft;
- balloons/lift envelopes used to carry people;
- powered gliders;
- rockets used for human ascent;
- launch infrastructure;
- instructional material sufficient to reproduce human flight.

They may preserve captured instruments, engines, or fragments for study under seal.

Weather observation is not forbidden. In fact, Covenant anti-air crews are expected to understand wind and cloud precisely because the founding catastrophe involved ignored weather.

## The Unmoored

The Covenant calls aviation-centered outsiders **the Unmoored**.

This is not a racial or national category. In doctrine, anyone can become Unmoored by teaching severance, and anyone can theoretically return by renouncing it.

They regard the DrewCraft civilization's devotion to aviation—and especially the idea of an aviation spirit—as a dangerous inversion of their own faith.

Some Covenant scholars treat the aviation spirit as a literal deceiving presence. Others regard it as metaphor, propaganda, or mass enthusiasm. The state does not require agreement so long as flight itself is opposed.

## Death and burial

The Covenant treats burial as the completion of weight.

Bodies are returned to the ground, names are carved into stone, and the unreturned dead are represented by empty graves.

This tradition originates directly in the Falling Fire, when many victims and pilots could not be recovered.

The Red Reliquary is the most important regional expression of this doctrine.

## Pilgrimage and roads

Walking has religious significance because the body physically experiences distance, slope, fatigue, and terrain.

The Covenant does not oppose carts, roads, bridges, or rail-like ground infrastructure. Their objection is not to technology or speed in general; it is specifically to severance from the earth.

Surveyors and road builders have unusually high status.

This is also why their strategic coordinate system uses **divided waymarks**.

Ordinary couriers rarely carry a complete set of coordinates. Separate copies may carry X, Z, X+Y, Z+Y, or occasionally X+Z. Only senior documents normally contain all three axes.

That security practice becomes the in-game clue mechanic.

## Military doctrine

The Covenant prefers:

- fortified roads and passes;
- layered walls;
- patrols tied to regional source sites;
- heavy ground troops;
- clerics/scribes embedded with military units;
- anti-air batteries around major sites;
- overlapping defensive zones around Vespera.

Their anti-air doctrine is denial rather than perfect marksmanship.

Flak exists to make direct overflight dangerous, force aircraft to maneuver, and drive attackers toward the ground where Covenant soldiers can fight them.

The military texts explicitly acknowledge that altitude, maneuvering, terrain, cloud, and weather reduce anti-air accuracy.

## Internal character

The Covenant should not read as cartoonishly evil.

Its founding trauma was real. Its religious argument is coherent enough that ordinary believers can sincerely hold it. Its texts include family letters, burial customs, supply records, weather notes, and arguments among scholars.

At the same time, it has become authoritarian and expansionist. It burns aviation knowledge, attacks the Unmoored, hides strategic information, and organizes armed campaigns to prevent a competing civilization from normalizing flight.

Its tragedy is that a doctrine originally built around humility toward physical reality becomes a justification for controlling how everyone else is allowed to move through the world.

## Endgame relationship

The eight regional Source Cores are the gameplay expression of the Eight Chains.

Clearing a Chain:

1. permanently suppresses that strategic source;
2. activates that Chain's state on the Flightstone at spawn;
3. makes its sealed capital fragment reliably obtainable;
4. contributes to opening the frontier containing Vespera.

The ordinary dropped books point toward the eight named sites.

The eight sealed books point toward Vespera.

The Flightstone preserves the campaign record so lost books cannot erase progression, but the books remain the primary lore delivery mechanism.
